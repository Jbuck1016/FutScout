01 / Before kickoff

A narrow favourite is not a prediction of certainty.

The model gave Brighton a little over one chance in three. Arsenal were the most likely single outcome, but Arsenal not winning was more likely than Arsenal winning.

Log loss and multiclass Brier score evaluate the full probability forecast; lower is better. They are not percentages. One match cannot establish calibration or model superiority.

What pulled the forecast towards Arsenal?

Signed effects are the saved contributions to home-minus-away log goal rate, not literal goals or additive win-probability points. Positive favours Brighton. Last-three figures are context; the fitted model uses multiple windows. These are associations, not causal explanations.

02 / Match evidence

Territorial control and chances told different stories.

Arsenal led field tilt and completed pass entries into the box. Brighton led shot volume and the event-feed fitted xG total.

Those facts can coexist. Territory measures where possession develops; it does not guarantee that possession ends in good shots. Equally, this match cannot prove territorial features are useless.

06 / What we should learn

Investigate the mechanism. Don’t tune to one result.

  1. Verify the inputs. Raw-source identity is verified and the xG/xT definitions are reconciled; retain those contracts in future reviews.
  2. Inspect game state. Brighton’s lead may change later territorial patterns. Whole-match totals cannot distinguish cause from response.
  3. Test chance creation beyond territory. Compare prior-match shot quality, box entries, xT and pressing with consistent historical coverage and identical time cuts.
  4. Require repeatability. Judge changes on paired chronological holdouts and genuinely frozen future calls—not this one loss.

Keep the current shadow model unchanged while those checks run. The evidence supports a useful investigation, not a confident diagnosis of why Arsenal lost.
